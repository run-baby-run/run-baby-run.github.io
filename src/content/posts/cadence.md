Cadence is how many steps you take in a minute, counting both feet. Your watch
records it. Most new runners are somewhere between 150 and 165.

## Why anyone cares

Fewer, longer steps mean more time in the air, which means you land harder and
further in front of yourself. Every one of those landings is a small brake
followed by a push to get going again.

Quicker, shorter steps mean you land closer to underneath your body, with less
of a jolt each time. The distance covered is the same; the pounding is not.

## The 180 myth

You will read that 180 steps per minute is the target. It comes from someone
counting elite runners at the 1984 Olympics — who were racing, and who were
mostly small and fast.

Cadence properly depends on your height, your leg length and how fast you are
going. A tall person running easy at 6:30/km has no business at 180. **Your own
number plus five percent is a better target than anyone else's number.**

## How to change it

Do not think about it while running — thinking about your feet makes everything
worse. Instead:

1. Find your normal cadence on a recent easy run.
2. Add about 5% and pick music or a metronome at that rate.
3. Use it for **ten minutes** of an easy run, then forget about it.
4. Repeat for a few weeks.

It will feel ridiculous and slightly frantic for about two runs. Then it stops
feeling like anything, which is how you know it has taken.

## What not to do

Do not try to run faster by taking longer steps. That is
[overstriding](/blog/stride-length), and it is slower as well as harder on you.
Speed comes from pushing back against the ground, not from reaching forward.

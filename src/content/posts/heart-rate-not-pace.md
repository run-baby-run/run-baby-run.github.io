Run the same loop twice at the same pace, once on a cool March night and once in
August, and the second one will nearly kill you. The pace was identical. The
effort was not.

That is the case for heart rate in one sentence: **pace is an output, heart rate
is a cost.** Training works on the cost.

## Where pace lies

- **Hills.** There is nowhere flat in Yerevan. A 6:00/km up through Kond and a
  6:00/km back down are completely different runs.
- **Heat.** Above roughly 20°C your body starts spending blood on cooling you
  instead of on your legs. Same pace, higher cost.
- **Tiredness, bad sleep, a cold coming on, too much coffee.** All of it shows up
  in heart rate and none of it shows up in pace.

## The zones, briefly

Most watches split your range into five. For our purposes only two matter:

- **Zone 2 — easy.** You can talk. This is where most of your running lives.
- **Zone 4 and up — hard.** You cannot talk. This is where
  [intervals](/blog/intervals) live.

Zone 3 is the trap — the not-quite-easy, not-quite-hard middle that tires you out
without improving much.

## Getting your zones roughly right

Your watch guesses your maximum heart rate from your age, usually as 220 minus
it. That formula is **wrong by about 10–12 beats either way** for most people, so
treat the zones as a starting point rather than as physics.

Calibrate them against the talk test. If your watch says Zone 2 but you cannot
speak a full sentence, your zones are set too high — not your effort too low.
Trust the sentence.

## Two honest caveats

Heart rate **lags**. For the first five or ten minutes it is still catching up,
so ignore it early in a run.

And it **drifts** upwards over a long run even at constant effort, especially in
heat. That is normal, and it is information: your body really is working harder
to hold the same pace.

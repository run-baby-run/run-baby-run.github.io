This is the part that is hardest to believe when you are enjoying running:
**the run is the stimulus, not the improvement.** The improvement is what your
body builds in response, and it builds it while you are doing nothing.

Skip the doing nothing and you get the damage without the repair.

## Sleep is the big one

There is no supplement, stretch or gadget that comes close. Most of the
repair work happens during deep sleep. If you are choosing between an extra
forty minutes of running and an extra forty minutes in bed, in most weeks the bed
wins.

This is worth saying out loud in a club whose main run starts at **ten at night**.
If getting home at half past eleven wired and buzzing costs you an hour of sleep
twice a week, that is a real cost. Some of us are better off on Thursday
mornings, and there is no prize for the night run.

## Rest days

At least one a week, doing nothing at all. Complete rest — not a
[recovery run](/blog/recovery-runs), not "just a few kilometres".

If you are new, two are better than one. Running four days a week consistently
for a year will beat running six days a week for two months and then stopping,
which is what usually happens.

## Down weeks

Every third or fourth week, cut the distance by about a quarter. Full detail in
[adding distance](/blog/adding-distance). This is the same idea on a longer
timescale.

## When your body is telling you

Take an unplanned rest day if two or more of these show up together:

- Resting heart rate several beats higher than usual for a few days
- Legs still heavy three days after a hard session
- Sleeping badly despite being tired
- Losing interest in running you normally look forward to
- Getting every cold going round

None of these is a moral failing and none of them is fixed by trying harder.

An interval session is any run that alternates hard efforts with recovery.
Four minutes fast, two minutes jogging, five times over. That is it — the rest is
detail.

## What they actually do

Hard efforts push your body to improve the things easy running does not reach:
how much oxygen you can use per minute, how well you clear the by-products of
hard work, and — not least — how it feels to be uncomfortable and keep going.

That last one matters more than people admit. A race hurts. Intervals are where
you practise hurting on purpose, in small doses, with a clock telling you when it
ends.

## When to start

**Not in your first couple of months.** Intervals put the highest forces through
tissue that adapts slowest, so they belong on top of a
[base](/blog/base-training), not underneath one. If you have been running for six
weeks, keep running easy and come back to this.

## Start with strides

The gentlest entry, and the one our own plans use first: after an easy run,
**four to six efforts of about 20 seconds**, quick but relaxed, with a minute of
walking between. Not a sprint — think "fast and smooth".

Strides cost almost nothing to recover from, teach your legs to turn over
quickly, and make easy pace feel easier. They are in
[week one of the half marathon plan](/training/half-marathon-beginner-pace/) for
exactly that reason.

## Then proper intervals

When you want more, a first real session might be **5 × 3 minutes hard with 2
minutes jogging**. Hard means you could not hold a conversation and could hold
the effort for maybe ten minutes if forced.

Rules that keep it useful:

- **Once a week.** Twice is for people with years behind them.
- **Warm up properly** — at least ten minutes easy first, or the first rep is
  wasted and the risk is highest.
- **Even is better than heroic.** If the last rep is much slower than the first,
  you started too fast. A session where every rep matches is worth more than one
  that starts brilliantly and falls apart.
- **[Cool down](/blog/cool-down)** afterwards.

## The day after

Easy, or nothing. A hard session followed by another hard session is two
mediocre ones, and the fitness lands in the gap between them, not during.

"Base" is just the word for the months of ordinary running that everything else
is built on. It is mostly [easy running](/blog/easy-running), it is mostly
unremarkable, and it is the part people skip.

## Two clocks

The reason it exists is that your body adapts on two different timescales.

Your heart and lungs improve fast — noticeably within **four to six weeks**.
Tendons, ligaments, bone and cartilage improve slowly, over **months**. They have
less blood flow, so they remodel at their own pace and will not be hurried.

This mismatch is the single most common way beginners get hurt. Six weeks in you
feel great, so you run more and faster, and the parts that were not consulted
start complaining. The fitness was real. The structure underneath it was not
ready.

## What base training actually looks like

- Three to five runs a week, nearly all easy
- Distance creeping up [slowly](/blog/adding-distance)
- No intervals, or only [strides](/blog/intervals) — short, fast, over before
  they cost anything
- One longer run a week that grows over time

Eight to twelve weeks of that is a base. For a first half marathon, the
[twelve-week plan](/training/half-marathon-beginner-pace/) is essentially a base
with a race at the end of it.

## It is not wasted time

Easy running is not the price of admission for the interesting training. It *is*
the training. It builds the capillary network that delivers oxygen, the
mitochondria that use it, and the habit of turning up on a Tuesday when it would
be easier not to.

The people in this club who run well have almost all been running unremarkably
for a long time. There is no version of this where you skip that part.

There is more nonsense written about running form than about anything else in
the sport. Most of it is a coach describing one elite runner's habit as though it
were a law.

The honest position: your natural form is mostly efficient for your body, big
deliberate changes often make things worse, and there are about three things
worth attending to.

## 1. Run tall

Lean very slightly forward **from the ankles**, not from the waist. Bending at
the waist shuts down your hips, which are your engine.

The cue that works: imagine a string pulling you up from the crown of your head.
Chest open, eyes about twenty metres ahead rather than at your feet.

## 2. Land under yourself

The single biggest one, covered properly in
[stride length](/blog/stride-length). Foot down under your hips, knee soft, not
reaching out in front. Fix it by raising your [cadence](/blog/cadence) rather
than by thinking about your feet.

## 3. Relax everything above the waist

Tension costs oxygen and buys nothing.

- **Hands loose**, as though holding a crisp you don't want to break
- **Shoulders down** and away from your ears — check every few kilometres, they
  creep up when you get tired
- **Arms swinging front to back**, not across your chest; elbows around 90°
- **Jaw and face slack.** Sounds silly, works.

## What not to worry about

**Whether you land on your heel or your midfoot.** Where your foot lands relative
to your hips matters. What part of it touches first mostly does not, and
deliberately switching has injured a lot of people who were fine before.

**Arm carriage, hip rotation, and the rest.** If you are not in pain and not
falling over, leave it.

## How to change form at all

Never mid-run for long, and never more than one thing. Pick a cue, hold it for
**thirty seconds at a time** a few times during an easy run, then let it go. It
is learning, not enforcing — and it takes weeks, not sessions.

Uphill running is the cheapest form coach there is: you cannot overstride going
up, so two minutes of any Yerevan hill does more than an hour of thinking about
it.
